Working Papers

Universal Cash Transfers and Income Inequality: Evidence from the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend

Read abstract

As automation and artificial intelligence reshape labor markets, unconditional cash transfers have gained attention as a potential policy response to rising income inequality. In this paper, I investigate the effects of universal cash transfers on income inequality, using the introduction of the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend in 1982 as a natural experiment. I focus on total household income inequality using data from the Current Population Survey. Using the synthetic control method, I find that Alaskan income inequality fell after the introduction of the dividend, a reduction of 0.02 in the Gini Index that is not statistically significant. I show that this decrease is consistent with the mechanical effect the policy should produce on its own, which suggests that behavioral responses have a negligible effect on income inequality.
