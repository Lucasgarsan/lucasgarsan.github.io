I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Southern Methodist University. I hold a master's degree in Economics from the Federal University of Pernambuco (UFPE) and a bachelor's degree in Economics from the State University of Santa Catarina.

I am an applied econometrician with a focus on policy and labor economics, studying how cash transfers and economic shocks shape household well-being. My current work uses the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend as a natural experiment to study the effects of universal basic income.

Featured Paper

Universal Cash Transfers and Income Inequality: Evidence from the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend

Using the 1982 introduction of the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend as a natural experiment and the synthetic control method, I find that Alaskan income inequality fell after the dividend, consistent with the policy's expected mechanical shift in the income distribution when negligible behavioral changes are present. Moreover, I show evidence that the 1986 Tax Reform Act confounded previous estimates of the dividend's effect on inequality.
